# Model Card — Customer Churn Prediction

## Model details
- **Model type:** Gradient Boosting Classifier (scikit-learn `GradientBoostingClassifier`)
- **Version:** `gradient_boosting_v1`
- **Hyperparameters:** `n_estimators=150`, `learning_rate=0.05`, `max_depth=3`
- **Framework:** scikit-learn 1.5, tracked with MLflow, versioned with DVC
- **Developed by:** Solo submission — MAI201NAA.06381.2264 (MLOps course project)
- **Date:** August 2026

## Intended use
Predicts whether a telecom customer will churn (cancel service) in the near
term, based on account and service attributes, so the retention team can
prioritize outreach. Intended for **educational / course-project use** — not
validated for real production business decisions.

**Out of scope:** Any use to deny service, set pricing, or make decisions
directly affecting a real customer without human review.

## Training data
- **Source:** Synthetic dataset generated by `src/generate_dataset.py`,
  designed to mimic the structure and statistical patterns of the public
  IBM/Kaggle "Telco Customer Churn" dataset (7,043 rows, 21 columns).
  **This is not real customer data.** See `docs/dataset_card.md` for full
  documentation of how it was generated and its limitations.
- **Size:** 7,043 rows → 5,634 train / 1,409 test (80/20 stratified split)
- **Features:** 19 features after dropping `customerID` and separating out
  the `Churn` target column (21 total columns − 1 identifier − 1 label) — a
  mix of
  demographic (gender, senior citizen, partner, dependents), account
  (tenure, contract, billing, payment method), and service usage
  (phone/internet/streaming add-ons) attributes.
- **Target:** `Churn` (Yes/No), baseline churn rate ≈ 38.7%.
- **Preprocessing:** Label encoding for categorical features (see
  `docs/dataset_card.md` for the documented ordinal-assumption limitation
  this creates for the Logistic Regression baseline); missing
  `TotalCharges` values imputed with the column median (the real Kaggle
  dataset has 11 such rows; this synthetic version has none, but the
  imputation step runs regardless so the pipeline is compatible with both).

## Evaluation results (test set, n=1,409)

All threshold-dependent metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1) use
scikit-learn's default **decision threshold of 0.5** on the predicted churn
probability. ROC-AUC is threshold-independent and reflects ranking quality
across all thresholds.

| Model                     | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1 score | ROC-AUC |
|----------------------------|----------|-----------|--------|----------|---------|
| Logistic Regression (baseline) | 0.759 | 0.708 | 0.645 | 0.675 | 0.829 |
| Random Forest (experiment 1)   | 0.759 | 0.715 | 0.626 | 0.668 | 0.827 |
| **Gradient Boosting (experiment 2 — selected)** | **0.762** | **0.720** | **0.632** | **0.673** | **0.838** |

Confusion matrix (selected model, threshold=0.5): see `reports/evaluation.json`.
Full experiment tracking: `mlflow.db` (MLflow UI: `mlflow ui --backend-store-uri sqlite:///mlflow.db`).

## Metrics justification
ROC-AUC was the primary **selection** metric because churn prediction is used
to **rank** customers by risk for a limited-capacity retention team, not just
classify with a fixed threshold — the team can work down the ranked list
regardless of where the cutoff sits. **Recall** is reported and monitored
just as closely: since the business cost of missing an actual churner
(false negative) is generally higher than one extra retention call to a
customer who wouldn't have churned (false positive), a future iteration
could deliberately lower the decision threshold below 0.5 to trade some
precision for higher recall — the ROC-AUC would be unaffected by that
choice, which is exactly why it's used for model *selection* while
precision/recall/F1 are reported at threshold=0.5 for interpretability.

## Limitations & risks
- **Synthetic training data**: the model has never seen real customer
  behavior. Deploying it on real customers without retraining on real,
  consented data would be inappropriate.
- **Feature drift**: contract mix, pricing, and tenure distributions shift
  over time (see `reports/drift_report.html`); the model should be
  monitored and retrained regularly (see Phase 2 monitoring pipeline).
- **Fairness**: no fairness/bias audit across demographic slices
  (`gender`, `SeniorCitizen`) has been performed. This should precede any
  real-world use.
- **Class imbalance**: churn is the minority class; recall on churners is
  lower than precision would suggest — see `reports/evaluation.json` for
  the full classification report.
